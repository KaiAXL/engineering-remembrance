Five rounds. Five real records.

A case file from my grandmother Helen's search. Read the clues on the real documents, say how sure you are, and close the case.

Why this exists: most of us inherit names, not documents. Here you can practice reading real records before you meet your own family's.

These are real records of real people. Many of them were murdered. This exercise teaches you to read records like these, so you can find your own family.

The whole case file, with every answer

Round 1 · Reconstruct · Buchenwald · 2 June 1944

Read the card

Zacharias Helman, Helen's father. For years, all we looked at was his photo.

  1. Warm-up. Type his prisoner number, as it is on the card.

    57101. Searching a number like this in Arolsen is how I later found his nephew's card.

  2. The card says born 4.11.01. Which record do you trust for his birthday?

    11 April 1901, in the register made when he was born. The camp clerk swapped the day and the month.

Reconstruct Trust the record made at the time of the event, and note where later records disagree.

Arolsen Archives · Buchenwald prisoner card (Häftlings-Personal-Karte) 57101, Zacharias Helman

Round 2 · Correlate · Buchenwald · the intake list

Read the neighbors

The nominal roll of the thousand men who reached Buchenwald from Auschwitz on 2 June 1944, listed by prisoner number. Zacharias is lit up, at 57101.

  1. Zacharias is lit up. Which line would you look up next?

    Helman, Michal, 57103: Zacharias's nephew, with a photo of his own.

  2. Two pages earlier, page 6, there is one more Helman. Find him and type his prisoner number.

    56906: Helman, Lajos. 56892 at the top, 900 rolls the hundreds over, so 6 means 56906.

Correlate Read the whole list. Family travels together.

Arolsen Archives · nominal roll of the 1,000 Hungarian-Jewish arrivals at Buchenwald, 2 June 1944, roll dated 10 June 1944, pages 8 and 6, scans 0002077 and 0002075

Round 3 · Verify · Buchenwald · registration forms

Were they brothers?

Lajos and Michal Helman, registered the same day.

  1. Lajos and Michal: which line matches on both forms?

    House 330 on both forms, with the same trade and the same village.

  2. The mothers' first names differ: Hany and Wilhelma. With what you've found, were they brothers?

    Yes. The strongest anchor: the mothers' maiden names agree. Lajos's form says Petranker; Michal's reads Pelranka, a variant of Petranka that belongs in the name matrix. Same house, trade and village too. One first name that differs isn't enough to reject a match.

  3. Two civil birth records, from Bohdan's registrar, not a camp. Which surname do both give for the mother?

    Petranker, on both. Two boys born nine months apart in Bohdan, both to Hany Petranker: Lajos and Michal were brothers, now on an independent kind of record. One person, several names: Lajos is Leib, Michal is Mordko. A 'Helman' search misses Mordko, because his father's field is blank and the index files him under his mother's name.

Verify Don't reject a match on one field. Anchor on several together.

Arolsen Archives · Buchenwald registration forms, 2 June 1944, prisoners 56906 (Lajos Helman) and 57103 (Michal Helman)

Round 4 · Normalize · Kloster Indersdorf · March 1946

What's wrong with this DP card?

Helen's registration at a children's DP center in 1946. Under it, her civil birth record from 1929.

  1. Helen's birth record says 13 April 1929. Does the date in box 6 of her DP card agree?

    20-IV-1931. On paper she was two years younger.

  2. Here is a typed transcription of Helen's entry. One reading is wrong. Whether a person or an AI typed it, check it against the scan: which field is wrong?

    Yes: the number. AI reads, you check. Now, what does it really say?

  3. Compare the DP card with her birth record under it. Which part of the DP card agrees with the birth record?

    Helman Zacharia and Laundav Rozi: both parents named, and both agree with her birth record. That's how I knew it was her.

Normalize On DP papers, trust the parents' names first.

Arolsen Archives · A.E.F. D.P. Registration Record, Kloster Indersdorf, 27 March 1946 (Arolsen 67988803)

Round 5 · Verify · My research log

Keep, set aside, or cross off?

Leads that looked right when I found them. Sort them.

  1. A passport for a Dávid. I was looking for Zacharias's brother, David Helman, born 1902. A passport file for a “Dávid” turned up in the Uzhhorod archive, right era. The finding aid: David Auslender, born 1907, emigrating to New York. Zakarpattia regional archive, Uzhhorod · Fond 12, Opis 2, Delo 392

    Where does this lead belong?

    Crossed off. A different surname and a different birth year: someone else. Our David went east.

  2. A birth record for Roza?. Helen's mother is proven: Landó Rozi on Helen's birth record, Laundav Rozi on her DP card. What I didn't have was Roza's own birth record, the one that would name her parents. A Roza Landa, born 1911 in Uglya, looked like it, and was my best candidate for a long time. Then the birth records of Helen's brothers and sisters gave their mother as Ruchla Landa, of Ganya, not Uglya. Bohdan civil birth registers, 1934 to 1938 · Fund 1606, Opus 9, Delo 321 and 354 · Uglya births, Delo 385

    Is the Uglya birth record Roza's own?

    Set aside. The siblings' records point to Ganya, but pointing elsewhere isn't ruling out. I kept this one on the list for a long time.

Verify Probable is not proven. When you can't prove it or rule it out, set it aside: it's hard, and that's allowed.

From my research log. 1921 census, Ganya, house 48 (Hungaricana); each card names the document behind it.
