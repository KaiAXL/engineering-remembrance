Archive detective

Five rounds. Five real records.

A case file from my grandmother Helen's search. Read the clues on the real documents, say how sure you are, and close the case.

Why this exists: most of us inherit names, not documents. Here you can practice reading real records before you meet your own family's.

These are real records of real people. Many of them were murdered. This exercise teaches you to read records like these, so you can find your own family.

Five rounds · twelve clues · 20 minutes to an hour, depending on how closely you read · stop anytime and pick up where you left off

Best on a computer, like real research. Works on a phone too.

The whole case file, with every answer

Round 1 · Reconstruct · Buchenwald · 2 June 1944

Read the card

Zacharias Helman, Helen's father. For years, all we looked at was his photo.

  1. Warm-up. Type his prisoner number, as it is on the card.

    57101. Searching a number like this in Arolsen is how I later found his nephew's card. And notice whose card this is: KL Weimar-Buchenwald, top left. Auschwitz is where he was admitted first, on 26 May 1944; the Überstellt column beside it says he was moved to Buchenwald on 2 June 1944.

  2. The prisoner card says born 4.11.01, read the German way as 4 November 1901. Which record do you trust for his birthday?

    11 April 1901, in the register made when he was born. The camp clerk swapped the day and the month. I started with JewishGen too. The index is easier for a beginner, and the real record holds more. Learn both: the index helps you find them, the scan proves it.

Reconstruct Trust the record made at the time of the event, and note where later records disagree.

Arolsen Archives · Buchenwald prisoner card (Häftlings-Personal-Karte) 57101, Zacharias Helman

Round 2 · Correlate · Buchenwald · the intake list

Read the neighbors

The nominal roll of the thousand men who reached Buchenwald from Auschwitz on 2 June 1944, listed by prisoner number. Zacharias is lit up, at 57101.

  1. Zacharias is lit up. Which line would you look up next to learn about his family?

    Helman, Michal, 57103: Zacharias's nephew, with a photo of his own. A surname you know a line or two away is worth checking first: families travelled together and were registered together.

  2. Two pages earlier, page 6, there is one more Helman. Find him and type his full prisoner number: five digits.

    56906: Helman, Lajos, one more Helman on the same roll. Whether he is family, round 3 will test. 56892 at the top, 900 rolls the hundreds over, so 6 means 56906.

Correlate Read the whole list. Family travels together.

Arolsen Archives · nominal roll of the 1,000 Hungarian-Jewish arrivals at Buchenwald, 2 June 1944, roll dated 10 June 1944, pages 8 and 6, scans 0002077 and 0002075

Round 3 · Verify · Buchenwald · registration forms

Were they brothers?

Lajos and Michal Helman, registered the same day.

  1. Lajos and Michal: which line matches on both forms?

    House 330 on both forms, with the same trade and the same village.

  2. The mothers' first names differ: Hany and Wilhelma. With what you've found, were they brothers?

    Yes. Count what agrees: a small village, the same house, 330; born less than a year apart; the same surname and the same trade; and the mother's maiden name, Petranker on one form and Pelranka on the other, a variant that belongs in the name matrix. The first names differ because one clerk wrote a secular name and the other a Yiddish one: on real documents Adél Petranker appears in fifteen forms, among them Hany, Hani, Adél, Wilhelma, Edel and Ada. One first name that differs isn't enough to reject a match.

  3. Two civil birth records, from Bohdan's registrar, not a camp. Which surname do both give for the mother?

    Petranker, on both. Leib is Lajos: born 9 December 1924, the date on his camp form. But is the Mordko row really Michal?

    Is Mordko Michal? Match the fields, row by row.

    Michal on the camp records · Mordko on the birth index

Lajos and Michal were brothers, now on an independent kind of record. A 'Helman' search misses Mordko: his father's field is blank, so the index files him under his mother's name. Add Mordko to Michal's row of the matrix.

Their parents. Their father: Miksa (Mišel, Misael, Miska) Helman. Their mother: Hany Petranker.

Verify Don't reject a match on one field. Anchor on several together.

Round 4 · Normalize · Kloster Indersdorf · March 1946

What's wrong with this DP card?

Helen's registration at a children's DP center in 1946. Under it, her civil birth record from 1929.

  1. Helen's birth record says 13 April 1929. Does the date in box 6 of her DP card agree?

    20-IV-1931. On paper she was two years younger.

  2. Here is a typed transcription of Helen's entry. One reading is wrong. Whether a person or an AI typed it, check it against the scan: which field is wrong?

    Yes: the number. AI reads, you check. Read the left margin: each entry's number sits at the bottom of its entry, beside the year. Down the page: 79, then an entry crossed out, numbered 80; then 80 again, Anna's; then Helena's; then 82. Now, what does her number really say?

    What is her real entry number? Type it.

    81. The 1 looks like a 7, and the page settles it: 78, 79, 80 crossed out, 80, 81 Helena, 82. I read it as 87 at first, and fixed it from the full page. AI reads, you check, against the scan, every time.

  3. Compare the DP card with her birth record under it. Which box ties the card to her, so you know it is her? Tap it.

    Helman Zacharia and Laundav Rozi: both parents named, and both agree with her birth record. That's how I knew it was her. And why her birth record gives her no surname of its own: her parents had married religiously only, with no civil marriage, so the state registered her under her mother's name, and her father acknowledged her in the margin note. Landó-ová is Landó with the Czech feminine ending, -ová.

Normalize On DP papers, trust the parents' names first.

Arolsen Archives · A.E.F. D.P. Registration Record, Kloster Indersdorf, 27 March 1946 (Arolsen 67988803)

Round 5 · Verify · My research log

Keep, set aside, or cross off?

Leads that looked right when I found them. Sort them.

  1. A passport for a Dávid. I was looking for Zacharias's brother, David Helman, born 1902. A passport file for a “Dávid” turned up in the Uzhhorod archive, right era. The finding aid: David Auslender, born 1907, emigrating to New York. Zakarpattia regional archive, Uzhhorod · Fond 12, Opis 2, Delo 392

    Where does this lead belong?

    Crossed off. A different surname and a different birth year: someone else. Our David went east.

  2. A birth record for Roza?. Helen's mother is proven: Landó Rozi on Helen's birth record, Laundav Rozi on her DP card. What I didn't have was Roza's own birth record, the one that would name her parents. A Roza Landa, born 1911 in Uglya, looked like it, and was my best candidate for a long time. Then the birth records of Helen's brothers and sisters gave their mother as Ruchla Landa, of Ganya, not Uglya. Roza's own birth entry has never been found, so her children's records, each naming their mother, were the best evidence of who she was. Uglya is about 15 km from Ganya, Roza's maternal village, in the same Tiachiv district: close enough that a Roza Landa from there was a serious lead. Bohdan civil birth registers, 1934 to 1938 · Fund 1606, Opus 9, Delo 321 and 354 · Uglya births, Delo 385

    Is the Uglya birth record Roza's own?

    Set aside. The siblings' records point to Ganya, but pointing elsewhere isn't ruling out. I kept this one on the list for a long time.

    The 1921 census, Ganya. Then I read the 1921 census. In Ganya, house 48: Simon Lebovics, his wife Sura Landa, and their daughters, Ruchlya among them. And the Uglya Roza's own birth record names her mother as a Perl. In 2008 Helen named her mother's sisters, Malka and Gittle; this house lists Malka and Gitla among the daughters. 1921 census of Subcarpathia, Máramaros county, Teresva district, Gánic (Ganya), house 48 · Hungaricana, pages 133–134 · Uglya births, Delo 385

    Now: is the Uglya birth record Roza's?

    Crossed off. Helen's mother was Roza Landó, proven by Helen's birth record and her DP card, and she grew up in Sura Landa's house in Ganya. The Uglya Roza was someone else. Set aside first, crossed off once the census was read.

Verify Probable is not proven. When you can't prove it or rule it out, set it aside: it's hard, and that's allowed.
