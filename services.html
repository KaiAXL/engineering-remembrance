Getting started

Introductory call

Free

About 30 minutes, longer if needed, on what you already know, what you have at home, and which records are likely to exist for your family’s towns and years. You leave with a clear idea of what is possible, whether or not we work together.

Research assessment

$400

A first search of the main archives for one person, usually about four hours, then a short written report and a call on what exists and what to do next. It is the right first step when you are not sure how much is out there. If your family has a large collection to review first, I quote the assessment after seeing it.

The fee is credited toward research booked within 60 days: an assessment plus a first block is $990, not $1,390.

Research

Records research, by the hour

$99 / hour

Searching, reading, translating and verifying records with AI across 26 databases and 12 languages, billed by the hour, because nobody can know in advance how much has survived or where it leads. Research runs in blocks of 10 hours ($990), each approved and paid before it starts, and I never go past a block without asking. Your written quote states the first block and the expected total before you pay. At the end of each block I show you what I found and what is still worth doing, and you decide whether to continue. Hours you don’t use are credited to the next block or refunded.

  • Each name searched in the spellings and alphabets it was recorded in.
  • Cousins, neighbors, fellow passengers and people in the same trade, who often lead to the family.
  • Small sources the usual searches miss: city directories, court and property notices, trade journals, local newspapers.
  • One person’s path stage by stage, from birth and family records through the war and, for survivors, the displaced persons years; or a whole family, with the scope agreed in writing. Living relatives are contacted only with your consent.
  • The findings build into the document package (below), delivered when the research ends. Identifying people in photographs is quoted separately.

The document package

No separate fee; built within your research hours

Every research job ends in one printable file that anyone in the family can read and check: who they were, told through the records; each record’s image, transcription, translation and citation, numbered so every finding points to its document; a timeline, a simple map and every spelling of each name (the name matrix); and what is still open.

How I work

  1. We talk first. A free call about what you know and what you hope to learn.
  2. I look at what you have. Your documents, photos, trees and family stories are the starting point, and I read what you send before I quote.
  3. You get a written quote. Scope, timeline and price, with a short agreement. Payment for the first block of research hours, or a 50% deposit for a story map or design, starts the work.
  4. I research. Archives in several countries and languages, with every finding checked against the original document wherever it survives.
  5. We check in. A 30-minute call every other week during research, skipped when there’s nothing new, and a walkthrough at the end of each research block. Both count toward your research hours. You hear about a slow archive or a surprise when it happens.
  6. You receive the evidence. The document package, in one printable PDF, so anyone in the family can see exactly what each finding rests on.
  7. I build what you chose. A tree, a map, an emblem, a website or a game. The balance is due on delivery.

Terms in brief

  • Quotes. A written quote and a signed agreement come before any work begins. Quotes are valid for 60 days. I take on a few clients at a time, and your start date is in the quote. This is a summary; the signed agreement governs.
  • Research. Each 10-hour block is paid before it begins. Unused hours go to the next block or are refunded at the end.
  • Story maps and design. A 50% deposit starts the work and is non-refundable once work begins. The balance is due on delivery, when the finished files are released. Larger projects can run in phases, each with its own deposit. If you stop partway, you pay for the work done, and never less than the deposit. One round of revisions is included within 30 days of delivery; further changes are billed hourly.
  • Payment. Prices are in US dollars, plus 4.712% Hawaiʻi general excise tax on work used in Hawaiʻi. Pay by check, bank transfer or card (card processing fee added). Payment details are on your written quote. Archive copy fees are passed on exactly; above $50 per research block, I ask you first.
  • Use. Family commissions are for personal and family use. A museum, a publication or any commercial use needs a separate license.
  • Ownership. Your document package, and your family’s own photographs, documents and recordings, are yours; you confirm you have the right to share them. Engineering Remembrance owns the code, designs and templates behind every map, website and game, which may not be copied or reused; you receive a license to use your finished work.
  • Findings stay tied to their evidence. Findings, grades, documents and citations may not be altered; only Engineering Remembrance makes changes, and I correct any error in my work at no charge. Any showing of the work credits Engineering Remembrance.
  • Privacy. Nothing about your project is published without your written permission, and personal information about living people never is. You choose whether I may show the finished work, show it with names removed, or not show it.
  • Discounts. Ask about discounts for Holocaust survivors, and for two or more maps, games or websites.
  • No guaranteed finds. Research is charged for the work, not for results: records don’t survive for everyone. Translations are for research and are not certified.

About me

I work in process engineering and am a third-generation researcher bringing data methods to Holocaust genealogy. From one prisoner card, I recovered the family of my grandmother, Helen Lando Helman, through 2,008 records from 26 archives and databases, in 12 languages. In October 2026 I presented it with Jude Richter of the U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum at the World Federation of Jewish Holocaust Survivors and Descendants conference in Philadelphia.
