Recommended Library

Other people’s books, films and memory work

Holocaust books, films and memory work by other people. Every one of them keeps a family’s story from being reduced to a name on a list, which is the same reason I do this.

Testimony · films

Eva Brettler

My grandfather Tibi’s niece

Liberated at Bergen-Belsen at eight, after Ravensbrück. She still speaks at Holocaust Museum LA and the Museum of Tolerance. Students have made films of her story, including the animated A Prayer for My Mother, scored by her grandson.

Why it’s here: she is family, and her story shows how testimony lives on: students retell it in their own films, and her grandson scored one.

Books · history

Zahava Szász Stessel

Snow Flowers (2009), Wine and Thorns in Tokay Valley (1995) and a story in Remember Us (2009)

Snow Flowers tells of the Hungarian Jewish women forced to build airplane parts at Markkleeberg, from camp diaries and survivors’ testimony. Wine and Thorns is the history of the Jews of her hometown, Abaújszántó. Her story “Stations on the Road of Life” in Remember Us (2009) is, besides Ibi’s book, the only first-hand account I know of MIKÉFE, the 1945 farm where Helen and Ibi were. Snow Flowers came out in German in 2021 as Schneeblumen.

Why it’s here: she was in my grandmother Helen’s group at Indersdorf, and stayed in touch with her in America.

Project · photographs

Remember Me? Displaced Children of the Holocaust

United States Holocaust Memorial Museum

About a thousand photographs that relief agencies took of surviving children after the war, to help find their families. More than a hundred are the name-card portraits taken at Kloster Indersdorf in 1945. The museum asks the public to help say who they are and what became of them.

Why it’s here: it is the same work I do, starting from a face and a name card. Jude Richter, who presented with me in Philadelphia, worked on it.

See the children

Museum · Bavaria

Augustiner-Chorherren-Museum, Markt Indersdorf

In the old monastery gatehouse, Marienplatz 1

A small museum at the monastery itself. Besides the canons’ history, it shows the war years and the UNRRA children’s center that ran there after the war, in photographs and documents.

Why it’s here: Helen was at Indersdorf in 1946. My brother Adam visited in September 2026 and found her group’s photograph on the wall.

Visit the museum (German)

Foundation · testimony

Zachor Holocaust Remembrance Foundation

Founded by Ben Lesser (1928–2025) and his daughter Gail Lesser-Gerber

Ben survived four camps and the death train from Buchenwald to Dachau, and told his story to students for years after. His most-watched talk, How I Survived Four Different Nazi Concentration Camps, has more than two million views.

Why it’s here: Ben passed through Buchenwald, where Helen’s father, Zacharias, was registered in 1944. His granddaughter Robyn Weber carries his work at Zachor; we met in Philadelphia.
